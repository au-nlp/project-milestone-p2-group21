\section{Datasets}
\subsection{\textsc{SMOL}}
\textsc{SMOL}\cite{caswell2025smolprofessionallytranslatedparallel} (Set of Maximal Leverage) is a high-quality translation corpus from English to more than a hundred low-resource languages. \textsc{SMOL} includes \textsc{SMOLSent} and \textsc{SMOLDoc}, which represent sentence-level and document-level translations, and they are accompanied by Gatitos\cite{jones-etal-2023-gatitos} which provides token-level translations across many of the same languages.
In this project specifically, we use the English-Cantonese portion of \textsc{Smol} and \textsc{Gatitos}, where there are translations on all three levels of data. For use in our experiments, we split the \textsc{SMOLSent} dataset such that there is a 80/10/10 split for training/validation/test. We make sure that all splits are completely distinct and are consistent across experiments. We only use training and validation during our experiments, saving the test set for a final evaluation of our models.

\subsection{Monolingual Cantonese corpus by M. Dare, et. al}
In their work on unsupervised Mandarin-Cantonese machine translation, Dare et. al.\cite{dare2023unsupervisedmandarincantonesemachinetranslation} collected a large corpus of monolingual Cantonese sentences from various online sources. In total 912258 lines were collected by scraping and cleaning data from Wikipedia, Instagram, and a few other sites including a few corpusses compiled for previous academic work. Common for most sources that form the corpus is that the content is mostly user-generated text, which often includes non-standard spellings and colloquial language, which is typical for Cantonese as a language.
We use this corpus to train Cantonese token embeddings for the embedding transform experiment described in Section 3.
